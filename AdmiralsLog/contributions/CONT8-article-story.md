# Summary plan for the article


Intro: models of science usually consider that science rewards people based on how much they produce but a lot of the reward is also due to how visible they are. 

We make a model to investigate that question. (here hopefully we have between one and three setup to focus on so people are not lost).

We test different values of curiosity and show that the problem is definitely there when a lot of people are interested by visibility more than anything. 

We introduce reproduction where people reproduce when they are good at visibility (or some visibility x prestige). And we show that if we make a darwinian evolution, PhDs are slowly shifting towards less curiosity. (this part we would need some more data to be sure that this basically lead the whole population slowly in a "bad" situation). 

We can then identify potential solutions against that effect (and put everything we investigate and deem interesting there, sabbatical, PhD lotteries on the researcher that gets the grant, varying the vanishment on prestige, making the PhD students more noisy, ... I don't think simulated annealing is a realistic situation)

And we round up talking about additional thing that could change a bit the results, like w,  and conclude on some potential policy options that would be cool in domains where this problem would be identified.
